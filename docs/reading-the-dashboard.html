Reading Perp Pulse

A pre-market radar for crypto perpetual futures. This guide explains every number on the screen — no knowledge of how it was built required.

Read time ~6 minutes Audience anyone looking at the screen Covers the matrix, the score, the badges, the limits
Start here

What this tool is for

Perp Pulse answers two questions, at two different moments. Everything on screen serves one of them.

Before the trading day (1–2 minutes). Scan the whole watchlist at once: which coins are leaning bullish, which are leaning bearish, which are sitting at a price level worth watching. This is the main grid.

When an alert fires (30 seconds). A price alert triggers on one coin. Tap its row. The question becomes narrower: is this dip a normal pullback, or is it a collapse in progress?

What it is not

It is not a trading bot, it does not place orders, and it does not tell anyone to buy or sell. It aggregates public exchange data into one screen. Every decision is still made by the person reading it.

The top strip

Three market-wide numbers sit above the grid, in a deliberately muted box. They are context only — they never affect any score.

ReadingPlain meaningRough intuition
BTC.DBitcoin's share of the total crypto marketRising = money crowding into Bitcoin, often at the expense of smaller coins
USDT.DStablecoins' share of the total marketRising = money sitting in cash, waiting. Often a risk-off tell
TOTAL3Total market value excluding Bitcoin and EthereumA proxy for how much money is in everything else

Beside them is an ETF button. It shows how much money flowed into or out of US spot Bitcoin ETFs on the last reported day. Tapping it cycles to a manual setting, for days when the source is unavailable.

The number

How the score is built

Five independent checks. Each one can add +1, subtract −1, or stay at 0. They are added together — nothing is weighted, nothing is hidden.

CheckWhat it looks at+1 when−1 when
ETFMoney into or out of US spot Bitcoin ETFsMore than $50M inMore than $50M out
FundThe fee traders pay to hold a position. Negative means short sellers are paying — a crowded downsideBelow 0%Above 0.015%
OIWhether new money is entering as price movesPrice up with new positions openingPrice down with positions closing
EMAPrice versus its 4-hour trend lineClosed above itClosed below it
SweepWhether price broke yesterday's high or low and then reversedBroke the low, then recoveredBroke the high, then failed
+3 to +5Clear to longConditions lean bullish. Look for entries in the lower half of the range.
−2 to +2Choppy / rangeNo agreement between the checks. Most days look like this.
−3 to −5Clear to shortConditions lean bearish. Look for entries in the upper half of the range.
A high score is not a signal to trade

+4 means four of five checks agree — nothing more. It says the background conditions lean one way. It says nothing about entry price, risk, or timing, and it cannot see chart structure. Treat it as one input, never as an instruction.

Screen order · 3

The panel that opens when you tap

Tapping a row opens a detail panel with two clearly separated blocks. They can disagree, and that is intentional — the single most confusing thing about this screen if nobody explains it.

BlockThe question it answers
BiasWhich way are conditions leaning overall?
Pullback healthRight now, is this dip orderly or is it a cascade?

Take a falling price where traders are closing positions. Bias calls that bearish — people are giving up. Pullback health calls it healthy — leveraged bets are being cleared out, which usually makes a violent continuation less likely.

Both readings are correct. They answer different questions. That is why they sit in separate boxes with their questions written above them, and why they are never added together.

Glossary

The badges and terms

TermMeaning
Open Interest (OI)How many positions are currently open. Rising = new money entering; falling = positions being closed
FundingA small recurring fee between traders. Positive = buyers pay sellers (crowded upside); negative = the reverse
Discount / PremiumPrice in the lower / upper half of its recent 4-hour range
Bull FVG 0.57%A price gap left by a fast move, 0.57% away. Such gaps often get revisited
TREND ↑ / RANGEWhether price is breaking to new levels, or oscillating in a band
PDL swept + reclaimedPrice dipped below yesterday's low and then recovered — often a sign the dip was hunting stop-losses
PDH swept + rejectedThe mirror image: poked above yesterday's high and failed
fresh shortsPrice falling while new positions open — active selling, not just people exiting
short coveringPrice rising because sellers are buying back, not because of new buyers. Weaker fuel
proxyOn the ETF chip: this is Bitcoin's ETF figure applied to another coin, because only Bitcoin has spot ETFs. It is background weather, not that coin's own data
Important

When not to trust the screen

Three things are worth checking before relying on anything shown.

An amber banner across the top

Data is more than 10 minutes old and the grid dims. A refresh failed. Do not read prices off a dimmed screen — this warning exists specifically because showing stale prices as if live is the worst thing this tool could do.

A row showing an error instead of numbers

That coin's data could not be fetched. The remaining rows are unaffected and still valid — failures are isolated to one row on purpose.

Chips reading ETF 0 or a value marked proxy

ETF flows publish once a day on US business days, so the figure is often stale or unavailable — and outside Bitcoin it is always borrowed. That check is the weakest of the five.

Two structural limits

Days are measured in UTC, so "yesterday's high" follows the exchange clock, not local time. And order-book readings are shallow — they cover roughly the nearest 1% of price and can be withdrawn instantly, so they are a hint, never a trigger.

Scope

What it deliberately leaves out

Some absences are choices, not gaps. There is no liquidation heatmap (no free source good enough to trust), no chart (that is what TradingView is for), and no alerting — the screen never pushes anything. It is opened deliberately, read, and closed.

The watchlist is fixed on purpose. A short, stable list is easier to know well than a long one that changes constantly.

Perp Pulse is a personal research tool for reading public market data. It is not financial advice, and it makes no recommendation to buy or sell anything.
